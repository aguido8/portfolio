01 / Off-Road suspension & shock mount

System requirements and geometry

Off-road suspension has to accommodate wheel travel and uneven terrain while maintaining controlled motion, mechanical clearance and durability. My work in Georgia Tech Off-Road Racing focuses on A-arm and shock-mount assemblies, particularly the transfer of shock reaction loads into the suspension structure.

I used SolidWorks to evaluate shock placement, wheel travel, joint alignment, kinematic behavior, obstacle clearance and the effect of geometry on load paths. Packaging decisions affect both suspension performance and the loads imposed on brackets and fasteners.

02 / Off-Road suspension & shock mount

Load cases and joint sizing

I modeled reaction forces and governing impact load cases in Excel. The shock load is resolved into reactions at the mounting interface, then used to compare candidate fastener sizes and mounting-tab geometry. The objective is to understand how the joint can fail before committing to a part geometry.

Shock-mount load path

  1. 01Wheel / terrainExternal reaction and impact loading
  2. 02SuspensionA-arm and shock geometry
  3. 03Shock eyeReducer and bolt interface
  4. 04Mounting tabsTransfer reaction into structure

The calculation framework compares single- and double-shear configurations. Double shear distributes the idealized transverse load across two shear planes; unequal stiffness, offsets and bending can prevent the real load split from matching that idealization.

τ = F / (n A)Nominal average bolt shear stress; n = number of shear planes
p = F / (t d)Nominal projected bearing stress at a loaded interface; use the reaction at that interface

These are representative sizing equations, not released design values. Additional checks include edge tear-out, material strength, factor of safety and geometry-specific stress concentrations.

03 / Off-Road suspension & shock mount

Shock reducer design and fabrication

I CAD-modeled and manufactured a shock-mount reducer that interfaces the shock eye with the mounting bolt and the available tab spacing. The reducer must locate the shock, establish the correct bearing contact and provide a repeatable assembly while remaining practical to machine.

Fit

Shock-eye bore, reduced inner bolt clearance and shoulder dimensions determine the locating interfaces.

Axial stack

A pair of reducers and the shock eye must fit between mounting tabs without creating unintended gaps or interference.

Preload

Clamp-load checks consider bearing/contact pressure and whether the reducer can withstand assembly preload without crushing or permanent deformation.

DFM

Material selection, step diameters, shoulder geometry, tool access, tolerances and inspection all inform manufacturable geometry.

04 / Off-Road suspension & shock mount

Finite element analysis and iteration

I applied ANSYS FEA to suspension-mount deformation, stress concentrations and load transfer under impact loading. The results helped refine component stiffness and joint geometry and compare likely failure modes with the analytical calculations.

For a meaningful comparison, the model should reflect how the part is restrained, where the force enters the assembly and whether the simplified load representation is appropriate for the real joint. Local peaks at a sharp corner or constraint require interpretation rather than automatic acceptance.

05 / Off-Road suspension & shock mount

Outcome and ongoing verification

The shock-mount work progressed from CAD and analytical load estimates to a manufactured reducer, with joint checks and finite element analysis supporting the design process. The portfolio does not state a final safety factor, material grade, bolt preload or competition qualification because the final approved values have not been documented here.

Ongoing priorities include checking the as-built stack-up, verifying actual bolt and material specifications, documenting preload limits and comparing the completed joint against the governing design load.
