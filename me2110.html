01 / ME 2110 autonomous robot

Competition and requirements

Our Pirates of the Caribbean-themed competition required an autonomous robot capable of completing multiple scoring tasks within a constrained environment. In the first sprint, the team focused on autonomous forward driving, cannonball delivery and knocking over mermaid targets.

I contributed as a designer and programmer, translating customer needs into measurable engineering specifications using a House of Quality and weighted concept-evaluation matrices. We compared concepts by scoring potential, reliability, cost, manufacturability and assembly effort rather than selecting mechanisms by novelty alone.

NeedEngineering translation
Reliable autonomous movementRepeatable start, drivetrain alignment and consistent motion
Deliver cannonballsControlled release and unobstructed feed path
Contact targetsArm reach, compliance and repeatable deployment
Build within constraintsBudget, motor packaging, fabrication time and ease of assembly

The early sprint targeted a cost below $120 and an average score of at least 60 points. These were design targets, not reported final competition results.

02 / ME 2110 autonomous robot

Mechanical architecture

The robot combined a 1:1 drivetrain, 3D-printed tires, a helical-gear axle, a solenoid-actuated cannonball release and compliant target-contact arms. These mechanisms had to coexist within the chassis while preserving access for assembly and changes between sprints.

Initial autonomous architecture

  1. 01StartAutonomous command sequence
  2. 02Drive1:1 drivetrain and wheel assembly
  3. 03ReleaseSolenoid pulls a retaining pin
  4. 04ScoreGravity-fed balls and compliant target arms

Drivetrain

Direct 1:1 transmission prioritizes simple packaging and predictable motion; axle fit and gear support affect reliability.

Cannonball mechanism

A V-shaped gravity-fed ramp holds the balls until a solenoid retracts the retaining pin. The release must avoid binding and interference.

Compliant arms

Laser-cut spring-like wooden geometry provides compliance for target contact; flexibility and repeatability both matter.

Chassis integration

Motor locations, travel paths, wire routing and assembly clearances must be considered together rather than independently.

03 / ME 2110 autonomous robot

Design for manufacturing and tolerances

I worked on iterative Onshape assemblies and evaluated gear/axle fits, laser-cut chassis features, 3D-printed components, motor packaging and clearances. A CAD assembly that fits perfectly on screen can behave differently after fabrication, especially when multiple tolerances accumulate across supports.

Manufacturing considerations included laser-cut feature accuracy, printed-part dimensional variation, axle alignment and clearance for moving mechanisms. We used assembly and testing feedback to identify areas where the model needed revision.

04 / ME 2110 autonomous robot

Autonomous programming and integration

I programmed autonomous sequences that coordinated drivetrain motion and mechanism actuation. Timing, mechanical travel and the state of each mechanism affected whether a nominally correct sequence actually produced a successful scoring action.

Testing integrated behavior is different from testing individual parts: a ramp can release correctly on a bench while failing when the robot changes orientation or the ball path is obstructed. We used repeated trials to diagnose interactions between the mechanical and programming subsystems.

05 / ME 2110 autonomous robot

Failure analysis and iteration

Repeated testing exposed gear fractures, axle interference and inconsistent actuation. I used root-cause analysis to separate mechanical fit, loading and mechanism behavior from programming symptoms. The investigation focused on understanding why a fault occurred before changing geometry or timing.

Observed issueInvestigation focusEngineering response
Gear fractureLoad path, support, tooth engagementEvaluate geometry and drivetrain alignment
Axle interferenceClearance and accumulated fit tolerancesCheck actual assembly against CAD
Inconsistent actuationMechanical travel, friction and timingIsolate mechanism behavior and repeat trials

The response column describes the diagnostic approach, not a claim that every failure was fully eliminated.

06 / ME 2110 autonomous robot

What the project demonstrated

The team integrated a multi-mechanism autonomous robot through requirements definition, concept selection, CAD, fabrication, programming and testing. My contributions demonstrate the link between design intent and practical mechanical behavior, especially when multiple components must work together reliably under time and cost constraints.
